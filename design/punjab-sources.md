# Punjab campaign references

## Territory and administration

The 1946 start governs the eastern districts of undivided British Punjab from a modeled works office in Jullundur. Lahore was the provincial capital. The map shows it in context across the western edge of the playable area. The Indian Independence Act established East and West Punjab in 1947. The campaign models the loss of western market access and allows investment in resettlement stores, relief convoys or district stock releases. Jullundur briefly hosted the East Punjab government, Simla followed, and Chandigarh formally opened on 7 October 1953. The game moves the administrative focus from Jullundur to Chandigarh in 1953 and depicts Simla as a hill district. [Indian Independence Act, section 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Geo6/10-11/30/section/4/enacted) · [Chandigarh district census handbook](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/324/download/946/DH_2011_0401_PART_A_DCHB_CHANDIGARH.pdf)

Patiala, Nabha, Jind, Faridkot, Kapurthala, Nalagarh, Malerkotla and Kalsia formed the Patiala and East Punjab States Union. The inauguration was on 15 July 1948 and the covenant took effect on 20 August. PEPSU was a separate administration with its own budget until the 1956 Act merged it with Punjab on 1 November. The player can fund a technical school at integration or retain cash for later cotton, canal and reserve programmes. [Government of India archive on PEPSU](https://eoibratislava.gov.in/public/upload_document/1751890853.pdf) · [Supreme Court judgment, paragraph 18](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2011/4799/4799_2011_9_1502_71625_Judgement_27-May-2026.pdf) · [States Reorganisation Act, section 11](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1680/1/a1956-37.pdf)

On 1 November 1966 the Punjab Reorganisation Act created Haryana and the Union territory of Chandigarh and transferred additional hill districts to Himachal Pradesh. Sections 2 and 49 allocate 54.84% of cash and bank balances to Punjab, 37.38% to Haryana and 7.78% to the Union for Chandigarh and the transferred territory. Local works follow their sites under the asset rules. The Bhakra and Beas projects have separate rules for rights, liabilities and shared operation. A Punjab or Haryana continuation therefore changes the treasury and the portfolio of local works while allowing each administration to benefit from the shared water and power system. [Punjab Reorganisation Act, sections 2–6, 48–49, 53–54 and 78–80](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1645/1/196631.pdf)

The 1946 provincial figures come from the budget speech of 22 March. The opening development reference uses the speech’s ₹605 lakh budget provision for post-war schemes. The East Punjab development caps use ₹86 lakh for the 7½ months beginning in August 1947, ₹663 lakh in FY 1948–49 revised estimates and ₹740 lakh for FY 1949–50 after revision of expected Central lending. The period assessment attributes more than 80% of development spending to Bhakra–Nangal. The First Finance Commission separately reports special grants of ₹100 lakh in 1947–48 and ₹175 lakh each in 1948–49 and 1949–50. The East Punjab and PEPSU series use the Second Finance Commission’s updated figures from FY 1951–52 through FY 1955–56. Enlarged Punjab uses the first full-year budget after the merger as its 1956 proxy, then uses the Fourth Finance Commission’s revenue receipts for 1961–65. The Fifth Finance Commission also gives totals for two of those fiscal years on a different reporting basis. For the 1966 split, the game uses each successor’s first full-year total. The FY 1966–67 Punjab total mixes both administrations and the Haryana figure covers only five months. [Second Finance Commission, Appendix XIII Tables 1 and 12](https://fincomindia.nic.in/asset/doc/commission-reports/2nd-FC/digitized/2nd%20FC%20-Digitised.pdf#page=150) · [Fourth Finance Commission, Appendix VII Table 20](https://fincomindia.nic.in/asset/doc/commission-reports/4th-FC/digitized/4th%20FC-Digitised.pdf#page=198) · [Fifth Finance Commission, Tables 6, 55 and 57](https://fincomindia.nic.in/asset/doc/commission-reports/5th-FC/digitized/5th%20FC%20-%20Digitised.pdf)

The game allocates 0.5% of the historical revenue reference to the modeled annual works envelope and caps that envelope at 30% of the development-spending reference when available. The revenue Plan outlays used as later caps are ₹2,330 lakh for enlarged Punjab in FY 1965–66 and ₹1,472 lakh and ₹926 lakh for Punjab and Haryana respectively in FY 1968–69. The opening provision uses local funding because the 1946 source provides a total receipts figure for this calculation. Where only an award is available, the modeled mix reflects that award’s tax shares and grants. Other Central grants may have existed outside the award. Later RBI totals have no transfer breakdown in the table used, so the modeled mix carries forward the latest sourced same-government ratio. It carries the earlier total forward between fiscal reference years and identifies the underlying year in the Budget office. ## Bhakra–Nangal and the food economy

Preliminary dam work began in 1945–46 and Nangal work in 1946–47. Punjab, PEPSU and Rajasthan shared the capital commitment under a control board that included the Centre. The 1951 estimate of ₹156 crore covered the full project. The national project and a playable state's own investments in irrigation distribution, power lines or workshop connections have different costs and schedules. [Comptroller and Auditor General, audit of the Bhakra–Nangal project](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/state_accounts_report/account-report-AP1951-52andAR1953-063f88360d54b67-12090581.pdf)

Planners chose to deliver the canals early. The system reached fields in 1954, Ganguwal's first unit generated power in 1955, and the main dam was completed in 1963. The campaign compares early canals and powered pumps with earlier workshop power and later canals. Regional delivery depends on the year the player approves the works. The main dam appears on the map from 1963 regardless of the player’s regional distribution choice. The 1960 Indus Waters Treaty provides historical context. [Indus basin history](https://bbmb.gov.in/indus-basin.htm) · [World Bank treaty announcement](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/127721589378651773/pdf/Announcement-of-Indus-Water-Treaty-Signed-on-September-19-1960.pdf)

The Food Corporation of India supports a later shift from local reserves to interstate procurement and dispatch. The parliamentary review records 10.25 lakh tonnes of wheat dispatched on government account from Punjab and Haryana in May–July 1968, with storage and wagon bottlenecks. That observation supports programme effects for covered depots, handling and market access. Campaign food and freight volumes use simulation units. [Food Corporations Act, section 13](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1415/5/A1964-37.pdf) · [Lok Sabha Committee on Public Undertakings, 1971 review, paragraphs 5.8–5.9](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/764485/1/copu_05_12_1971.pdf)
