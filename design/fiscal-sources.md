# Historical Fiscal References

Amounts are nominal rupees. One crore is 100 lakh. A campaign turn beginning in 1950 uses FY 1950–51 as its fiscal reference when available. Historical receipts, expenditure, cash balances and grants are different measures.

## Bihar calibration

- FY 1946–47 development expenditure was ₹43 lakh in the accounts, and FY 1947–48 was ₹101 lakh. The later ₹462 lakh for FY 1948–49 was a revised estimate and ₹892 lakh for FY 1949–50 was a budget estimate. The road-and-building aggregate covers a portfolio of works. [Government of India, Provincial Development Programme, Bihar pp. 37–39, 47–48](https://dspace.gipe.ac.in/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10973/39679/GIPE-022067.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=3).
- Bihar's closing cash balance on 31 March 1947 was ₹107 lakh, with reserves reported separately. The game uses this as a scale reference for opening playable cash. [Expert Committee on Financial Provisions, Appendix B, reprinted in Constituent Assembly Debates, 4 November 1948, p. 80](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/762996/1/cad_04-11-1948.pdf).
- Bihar revenue is ₹25.95 crore for FY 1949–50 and ₹28.97 crore for FY 1950–51. The later Second Finance Commission series gives ₹28.30, ₹30.22, ₹33.25, ₹35.80 and ₹37.74 crore for FY 1951–52 through FY 1955–56. The later series supersedes the First Commission's provisional figure and budget estimate for 1951–52 and 1952–53 in the Bihar profile. [First Finance Commission, Appendix VIII](https://fincomindia.nic.in/asset/doc/commission-reports/1st-FC/digitized/1st%20FC%20-%20Digitised.pdf#page=153), [Second Finance Commission, Appendix XIII Tables 5(a)–5(e)](https://fincomindia.nic.in/asset/doc/commission-reports/2nd-FC/digitized/2nd%20FC%20-Digitised.pdf#page=160).
- FY 1961–62 through FY 1963–64 Bihar revenue receipts were ₹82.49, ₹93.37 and ₹100.00 crore. FY 1964–65 revised estimates were ₹108.63 crore and FY 1965–66 budget estimates were ₹119.34 crore. FY 1963–64 includes ₹23.38 crore in shares of Central taxes and ₹12.60 crore in grants. [Fourth Finance Commission, Appendix VII Table 20](https://fincomindia.nic.in/asset/doc/commission-reports/4th-FC/digitized/4th%20FC-Digitised.pdf#page=196).

## Orissa calibration

- The 31 March 1947 cash balance was ₹60 lakh, with ₹10 lakh of reserves recorded separately. Development expenditure was ₹69 lakh in FY 1946–47 and ₹170 lakh in FY 1947–48. The FY 1948–49 revised estimate was ₹637 lakh. The FY 1949–50 original budget estimate was ₹1,100 lakh before the reduction in central lending. [Expert Committee, Appendix B, p. 80](https://sansad.in/uploads/const_Assmbly_Debates_Volume7_4_November1948_64efedfedd.pdf?updated_at=20), [Provincial Development Programme, Orissa pp. 117–118](https://dspace.gipe.ac.in/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10973/39679/GIPE-022067.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=3).
- The opening uses FY 1949–50 revenue of ₹10.82 crore as a proxy for early turns. FY 1950–51 revenue was ₹10.31 crore. The later series uses Second Finance Commission accounts through FY 1955–56 and the Comptroller and Auditor General's accounts through FY 1960–61. Fourth Commission accounts record ₹46.13, ₹62.27 and ₹69.29 crore in FY 1961–62 through FY 1963–64. FY 1964–65 and FY 1965–66 use a revised estimate and a budget estimate respectively. [First Finance Commission, Appendix VIII](https://fincomindia.nic.in/asset/doc/commission-reports/1st-FC/digitized/1st%20FC%20-%20Digitised.pdf#page=154), [Second Finance Commission, Table 1](https://fincomindia.nic.in/asset/doc/commission-reports/2nd-FC/digitized/2nd%20FC-Digitised.pdf#page=150), [Orissa Finance Accounts, pp. 9–10](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/state_accounts_report/account-report-FA-1960-61-067ab2a766160c9-30223549.pdf), [Fourth Finance Commission, Table 20](https://fincomindia.nic.in/asset/doc/commission-reports/4th-FC/digitized/4th%20FC-Digitised.pdf#page=198).

## Translation to game rules

- Opening cash is a modeled 32% share of each closing-balance reference, rounded to whole lakh. Bihar starts with ₹34 lakh and Orissa with ₹19 lakh. The annual development envelope is 0.5% of the revenue reference, capped at 30% of reported development spending when an early spending record is available. This gives Bihar ₹13 lakh and Orissa ₹5 lakh in the opening year. For missing years the game displays the nearest prior reference, or the earliest available one for the opening. The historical revenue figure includes recorded transfers. The spendable treasury uses the modeled allocation.
- The regular transfer line uses the observed transfer share, or the last observed mix where the total is available without a breakdown. It is carved out of the development envelope. Negotiated grants and their matching contributions are separate one-off scenario transactions. The Second Finance Commission discusses representations to the Planning Commission and matching assistance. The game's funding windows, eligibility and amounts are designed choices. [Second Finance Commission, sections 20 and 26](https://fincomindia.nic.in/asset/doc/commission-reports/2nd-FC/digitized/2nd%20FC%20-Digitised.pdf#page=14).
- Individual construction prices remain scenario estimates. A Ranchi–Patna road diversion with a Barakar bridge was originally estimated at ₹13.50 lakh and revised to ₹27.77 lakh after changes in specifications. The Kosi flood banks and protective measures were estimated at ₹10.67 crore in 1953. These provide scale references for future corridor and regional projects. [Public Accounts Committee, Third Report, sections 75–76](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/4654/1/pac_2_3_v1_1958.pdf), [Parliamentary oral answers, 14 December 1953, columns 1187–1188](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2641391/1/01_V_14-12-1953_p19_p21_s6.pdf).
