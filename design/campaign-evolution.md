# Bihar Campaign Evolution

The [current design](current-design.md) records the proposed annual brief, food presentation options, and rules for shared programme families. Report readability comes before changes to the food model or new cabinet systems.

## Current player experience

The campaign should answer three questions quickly. What does this place need? What can I change with the money and time I have? What did my last decision make possible? The current opening helps with the first two. The annual report shows effects, and the next iteration should turn those effects into the next decision.

The opening briefing offers three regional programmes. Food and water aims for surplus and resilience. Transport and industry builds access before its workshop opens. Skills and prosperity accepts a slower payoff to improve later production. Approval funds the whole programme and schedules the works over several years. The works office handles their placement. Players can hold back for reserves, accelerate delivery at an extra cost or switch direction when the works office becomes available.

## Next playable evolution

**Make follow-on decisions react to outcomes.** A busy corridor can qualify for freight bids. Connected food stores make the 1957 Union reserve offer viable, and earlier harvests determine how much the state can afford to ship. The state chooses a reserve floor for the entire agreement. Carry this structure into a timed skills funding opportunity with requirements that reflect earlier education choices.

**Make the monsoon change the plan.** The Cabinet compares typical conditions with the forecast flood or dry scenario, including projects due that year and food in reserve. A shortfall before imports gives the player a basis for choosing which works to fund or whether to cap emergency purchases. Resolved floods mark irrigated sites where food was lost or saved. Seasons with typical weather and no completed works go straight to the annual brief. Early food procurement and crossing reinforcement remain possible follow-ons.

**Playtest the Kosi compact.** The chapter now offers three coverage and financing agreements, a flood response during construction and a record of losses prevented after delivery. Compare whether focused protection arrives early enough to justify its smaller footprint, and whether the wider Union option leaves enough cash to deal with an emergency. Use the [fiscal benchmarks](fiscal-sources.md) as the reference for future price calibration and sourced scope.

## Later evolutions

- **Alternate histories from visible causes.** An outcome should have a traceable chain. For example, repeated flood losses could change the terms available for a regional agreement, or a connected southern industry could improve the case for a joint investment. Use period institutions, funding constraints and geography to determine which proposals are plausible. Give players early warning and a way to respond.
- **Build at the scale of the decision.** Let players choose endpoints, coverage areas and funding terms when they change the outcome. Keep individual placement delegated. Evaluate whether the map explains where the player invested and which places were left out.
- **Give towns different roles.** The Economy layer shows operating schools, industry, food and water facilities, and freight terminals as distinct illustrations. Workshop production is recorded at the site, including market, Skills and power effects. The next iteration should show how towns grow, change their role and handle congestion as trade and settlement patterns change.
- **Make the source map trustworthy.** Integrate the [Bihar map corridor](map-production.md), then compare settlement positions, river courses and period boundaries against sources. Keep administrative changes separate from physical geography and preserve save identities through map updates.
- **Try a contrasting territory.** Start only after Bihar's opening, midgame and payoff form a coherent campaign. A second territory should change the choice of priorities through its geography, institutions and fiscal position.

## Acceptance for the next gameplay release

In the production build, play two openings until each unlocks a different opportunity. Check that the player can point to the places that benefited, explain the budget trade-off and identify what they want to do next. Ask how many turns felt repetitive and where the interface forced the player to guess. Carry that feedback into the project record before adding another tier of simulation.
