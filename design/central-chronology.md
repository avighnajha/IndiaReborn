# Central Provinces and Berar campaign chronology

The player starts in Nagpur in 1946. The central province stays in India at Partition. The cabinet’s relief choices represent possible provincial responses to displaced households and pressure on food distribution. [Indian Independence Act, section 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Geo6/10-11/30/section/1/enacted)

Fourteen Eastern States merge with Central Provinces and Berar in January 1948. Makrai joins in February. The campaign combines both dates in one chapter, with a choice about funding a technical school in Jagdalpur. The Republic’s First Schedule names the state Madhya Pradesh in 1950 and the administration remains at Nagpur. [White Paper on Indian States (1948)](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/a8/White_Paper_on_Indian_States_%281948%29.pdf) · [Constitution as adopted, First Schedule](https://www.legislative.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/07/88cca69e868e50b217f855be2fb8bdba.pdf)

On 1 November 1956, eight Vidarbha districts pass to Bombay. The new Madhya Pradesh adds Madhya Bharat (except Sunel), Vindhya Pradesh, Bhopal and Sironj. The capital moves to Bhopal. Sections 76–77 assign assets according to their location and cash according to the population ratio. The campaign estimates Bombay’s share at 35.8% using the 1951 totals for the eight districts (7,607,038 of 21,247,533). It applies the share to the playable works treasury and food reserves, with debts outside the current economic model. The campaign uses modeled demand and harvest changes for the new territory. A separate freight fee can carry trade through Nagpur after the transfer. [States Reorganisation Act, sections 2, 8–10, 76–77](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1680/1/a1956-37.pdf) · [1951 Census, Madhya Pradesh, Table A-I](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/30458/download/33639/23726_1951_GPT.pdf) · [Madhya Pradesh Department of Aviation](https://aviation.mp.gov.in/about-us)

The 1957–58 drought is the campaign’s fixed dry monsoon in 1957. The forecast marks the risk before the player commits the budget. The annual brief uses the same food coverage and weather attribution as other campaigns. [Madhya Pradesh Second Five Year Plan Progress Report for 1957–58](https://digitallibrary.niti.gov.in/items/d0503e16-f034-4c51-88bb-5e85288fd72e/full)

Bhilai’s first blast furnace opens in 1959. The playable state decision concerns a school or a supplier development package. The latter adds two output to each market-connected workshop in the Chhattisgarh plain. The plant’s existence and commissioning date follow history. The state investments, costs and output bonuses are modeled. [Steel Authority of India](https://www.sail.co.in/index.php/en/plants/about-bhilai-steel-plant)

The Chambal project was a joint undertaking of Rajasthan and Madhya Bharat, carried forward by the reorganized Madhya Pradesh. Gandhi Sagar appears from 1960. The player funds a distribution package with a lasting harvest or industrial effect. The terms, costs and delivery of this state package follow the campaign. In 1960, Maharashtra takes over Vidarbha and inherits existing works, agreements and joint projects.
