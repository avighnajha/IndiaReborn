# Central Provinces and Berar Programme Sources

## Harvests and water

The 1957–58 Madhya Pradesh annual development plan reports that only 21 of 382 lakh cultivated acres were irrigated in 1954–55. The state's Second Plan progress report records severe drought and falling agricultural production in 1957–58. The opening programme models irrigation and storage in the Nerbudda valley and Chhattisgarh plain. The Central Water Commission lists Tandula storage in Durg, the Mahanadi Main Canal in Raipur and pre-Plan tanks in Bilaspur. The Tandula programme extends field channels around an existing system. [Annual Development Plan 1957–58](https://digitallibrary.niti.gov.in/items/7482d009-eadc-4be6-ba0b-2dc55df6dfe4/full) · [Second Plan progress report](https://digitallibrary.niti.gov.in/items/d0503e16-f034-4c51-88bb-5e85288fd72e/full) · [Central Water Commission irrigation compendium](https://www.cwc.gov.in/sites/default/files/major-and-medium-irrigation-projects-india-compressed.pdf)

The Chambal agreement models Madhya Pradesh's local distribution commitments within a shared Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh project. Rajasthan's Kota district plan dates the completion of the Gandhi Sagar dam and Kota barrage to 1960. The Central Water Commission's archive reproduces the 1961 Madhya Pradesh decision describing a fifty-fifty split of common dam and barrage costs and a separate division between the irrigation and power sectors. The game offers two state investment priorities with modeled local funding and benefits. Both branches leave the shared basin project in place. [Kota district plan, Chambal Valley Project](https://dmrelief.rajasthan.gov.in/content/dam/dmr/files/documents/ddmp2025/Kota.pdf) · [Central Water Commission agreement archive, p. 144](https://cwc.gov.in/sites/default/files/legalinst-vol-iiipart1.pdf)

## Trade and training

The Nagpur gazetteer records cotton cultivation and textile employment. The 1941 occupational and industry tables cover the districts that later form Madhya Pradesh, with Berar, Nagpur and Chhattisgarh listed separately. Berar cotton is an early industrial investment whose sites pass to Bombay in 1956. The Narmada and Raipur market links and the inland processing workshops develop retained districts. Bastar and Malwa cooperative or market projects are scenario investments made available when their districts join the player’s administration. [Nagpur District Gazetteer, industries](https://gazetteers.maharashtra.gov.in/cultural.maharashtra.gov.in/english/gazetteer/Nagpur/ind1.html) · [Census of India, 1941 industry tables](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/31179/study-description)

The University of Saugor dates to 1946, and the Central Provinces and Berar government established the Government Engineering College at Jubbulpore in 1947. The engineering laboratories and university programmes model state expansion of higher education. The Jubbulpore agricultural research path draws on the later integration of agricultural research, extension and teaching at Jawaharlal Nehru Krishi Vishwa Vidyalaya in 1964. Its delivery year follows the player's investment. [Saugor university history](https://sagarno4hsgv.kvs.ac.in/en/about-kv/) · [Jabalpur Engineering College history](https://web.jecjabalpur.ac.in/about-us/about-jec) · [JNKVV history](https://jnkvv.org/short-history)

All programme prices are development commitments in nominal lakh rupees, calibrated to the campaign's available treasury. The construction schedule, site footprint and in-game food and output effects are scenario values.
