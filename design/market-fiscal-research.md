# AI market fiscal references

AI markets previously received a flat ₹1 lakh each year. They now use a small, explicitly modeled portfolio allocation derived from historical fiscal observations where available. These amounts fund a market's works and trade commitments, rather than representing an entire government's budget. Opening cash remains modeled and existing saves keep their carried balances.

## Existing regional references

The market model uses one tenth of the existing [regional works envelope](fiscal-sources.md), rounded to lakh rupees with a one-lakh minimum. That envelope normally uses 0.5% of revenue receipts and can be capped at 30% of a separately recorded development outlay. The AI market share is a game design choice. The references retain their fiscal year, territory, status and source.

Period profiles distinguish the two divisions of Punjab, three phases of Assam, composite and successor Madras/Andhra, Bombay and its successors, and Central Provinces/Madhya Pradesh. A record dated after the current turn is never used for market funding. Where the relevant period profile has no earlier observation, the model keeps a labeled ₹1 lakh allowance. It does not silently use a successor's future revenue to fund an earlier administration.

## Later revenue observations

[RBI's 2010 Handbook, Table 5](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=12315) reports revenue receipts in **crore rupees**. The first panel covers FY 1980–81 through 1988–89. The game imports only 1980–81 through 1985–86 and converts crore to lakh once by multiplying by 100. Missing cells remain missing. Existing regional records with the same fiscal year take precedence, since several retain more precision than this rounded table.

The additional series cover 23 represented administrations, including Himachal, the Jammu exchange's statewide reference, Sikkim, Manipur and Tripura. Examples from the first panel:

| Administration | FY 1980–81 receipts, crore | FY 1985–86 receipts, crore |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Himachal Pradesh | 292 | 519 |
| Jammu and Kashmir | 258 | 602 |
| Manipur | 111 | 226 |
| Tripura | 123 | 231 |
| Sikkim | 37 | 92 |

Direct revenue records use a modeled 0.05% portfolio share. For example, Manipur's ₹111 crore reference produces a ₹6 lakh market allocation in 1980. The Jammu exchange uses a small share of the statewide reference; that does not extend its simulated geographical scope to all Jammu and Kashmir. The RBI table has no Arunachal revenue figure for these years, so the game does not create one. Its first Mizoram revenue observation within the campaign is FY 1985–86; earlier blank cells are not zeros.

## Union territory Plan observations

The Ministry of Home Affairs reports **annual Plan allocations**, a different fiscal measure from revenue receipts. These records use a separately declared modeled 0.5% portfolio share, with the same rounding and minimum. Five-year totals are not treated as yearly budgets.

| --- | ---: | --- |
| Arunachal Pradesh, 1972–73 | 390.10 | Approved Annual Plan, MHA 1972–73, printed p. 42, PDF p. 50. |
| Delhi, 1972–73 | 4,100 | Same table. |
| Mizoram, 1972–73 | 437 | Same table. |
| Arunachal Pradesh, 1980–81 | 2,621 | Annual allocation stated as ₹26.21 crore, MHA 1980–81, printed p. 23, PDF p. 27. |

The original pages were visually checked after extraction. The 1972 table's aggregate heading and bottom total differ; the game uses only the verified individual rows. ## Integration and remaining gaps

The selected allowance enters cash once, before an AI commits works, and is preserved in that year's report. Nearby governments and distant markets follow the same funding rule. Workshop, treaty, food trade and freight earnings remain separate receipts. A source update affects future turns without rewriting archived reports. The source, measure and fiscal year can be expanded in Neighbors and the market ledger.

Early Manipur and Tripura references and some other early administrations remain unrecorded. Older observations are carried forward openly where no later reference is present. Prices, opening cash, portfolio shares, reserves and project effects remain game models. Further historical calibration and campaign balance review are still required; this change does not claim a complete reconstruction of every state account for every year.

The later revenue series is now shared with the matching final-period player profiles. Bihar, Orissa, West Bengal, Maharashtra, Gujarat and Uttar Pradesh gain the missing FY1980–85 observations; existing, more precise Punjab, Haryana, southern, Assam and Madhya Pradesh records retain precedence. Earlier territorial profiles do not inherit successor receipts. Player reports snapshot their source and modeled split when a year resolves. [The balance review](late-campaign-balance.md) found large existing treasury surpluses; period prices, operating commitments and later objectives still require a coordinated pass. A nominal budget increase does not establish meaningful spending choices.
