# Madras and Andhra campaign references

## Territory and cash balances

The 1946 campaign begins in the composite Madras Province. The Andhra State Act created Andhra on 1 October 1953 and transferred part of Bellary district to Mysore. The Act's Second Schedule placed property and goods with the administration receiving the relevant territory or office. Paragraph 2(2) apportioned the combined treasury and Reserve Bank balances among Madras, Andhra and Mysore in the ratio 62⅔ to 36 to 1⅓, with credit adjustments at the Reserve Bank. Paragraph 2(1) separately provided for the apportionment of proceeds of public loans raised from July 1953. The campaign applies the treasury ratio to available works cash. Population and stored food use separate gameplay assumptions. [Andhra State Act, sections 2–4 and Second Schedule](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2176/1/A1953-30.pdf)

On 1 November 1956, the States Reorganisation Act enlarged Andhra with districts of the former Hyderabad State and renamed it Andhra Pradesh. Madras gained the southern Travancore-Cochin territories described in section 4. The new Kerala incorporated Malabar and the Kasaragod taluk. Mysore received the other South Kanara territories specified by the Act. The 1959 boundary Act, effective 1 April 1960, adjusted the border between Andhra Pradesh and Madras. The map uses period census outlines as references for the political chapters. Madras became Tamil Nadu on 14 January 1969. [States Reorganisation Act, sections 3–7](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1680/1/a1956-37.pdf) · [Andhra Pradesh and Madras (Alteration of Boundaries) Act, 1959](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1409/1/195956.pdf) · [Constitution of India, First Schedule](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/19632/1/the_constitution_of_india.pdf)

## Fiscal anchors

Amounts below are in nominal lakh rupees. The March 1946 budget estimated ₹4,144.068 lakh in revenue, including ₹294 lakh from income tax. The campaign's ₹51 lakh opening treasury rounds the same budget's anticipated opening cash of ₹50.63105 lakh. The subsequently observed closing balance on 31 March 1947 was minus ₹56 lakh, with ₹2,918 lakh in reserve funds. The works budget opens at the original estimate and evolves with the campaign's investments. The 1953 planning turn uses FY1954–55 as the first full-year proxy for each successor. The Second Finance Commission reported ₹2,277 lakh for residual Madras and ₹1,122 lakh for Andhra in the second half of FY1953–54. These figures cover only six months. The Andhra State budget also recorded ₹245 lakh as its share of the July 1953 composite Madras loan, a separate capital receipt. The 1956 planning turn uses the first full post-reorganization year. Andhra Pradesh's FY1956–57 accounts combined twelve months of Andhra State with five months of Telangana, yielding ₹3,844 lakh. The composite FY1956–57 Madras budget was adjusted after November, with preliminary revenue of ₹5,485 lakh. The game uses Madras's following budget estimate on existing taxation and Andhra Pradesh's following revised estimate. For these reference years, the central shares come from the Second Finance Commission's projected award, while later anchors use the tax and grant components reported alongside the accounts where available. Between observations, the budget office carries forward the latest available fiscal reference and identifies the year and status. ## Water, power and geography

The Mettur reservoir was in service before 1946. Neyveli Lignite Corporation was established in 1956 and the first units of its original thermal power station began operating in 1962. The campaign models a state commitment to field channels and pump connections or to the works needed to supply industry. Player approval dates affect those regional schedules. [Central Water Commission, Mettur assessment](https://cwc.gov.in/sites/default/files/sedimentation-assessment-mettur-stanley-reservoir-tamil-nadu-through-remote-sensing-may-2020.pdf) · [Department of Public Enterprises, Neyveli profile](https://www.dpe.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/08/4d2065895dff5a2c1ff523ab2571d230.pdf) · [Comptroller and Auditor General, Neyveli audit](https://saiindia.gov.in/webroot/uploads/download_audit_report/2026/CAG---NLC-India-Limited-2025-English-FINAL-SIGNATORY-10-12-2025-0698c6a3b5d7e42.69686670.pdf)

The 1956 Andhra State budget treats the Krishna Barrage and drainage works separately from Nagarjunasagar, whose foundation was laid in December 1955. The 1968 budget records the first ceremonial release into the canals in August 1967. The campaign makes delta distribution and inland workshops available earlier and reserves the Nagarjunasagar canal programme for the long campaign.
