# National campaign coverage

Reviewed 27 November 2026 against the [original handoff, V0.7–V1.0](India_Alternate_Futures_Codex_Handoff.md#v09--india-wide-campaign-beta). The candidate beta roster contains nine authored 1946 starts. The national beta and release gates remain open.

## What is simulated

**Playable** means a founding campaign with programmes, political choices, fiscal references, a regional map, saves and an ending. **Market** means an administration has its own harvest, reserves, money and investments under Standard politics. **Territory** means a place is included in ownership changes or historical geography, without an independent annual economy. These categories overlap: a playable region uses the full campaign model when selected and can be a market in another campaign. Light politics retains nearby trade and agreements and omits the national clearing network.

The atlas's **Beyond your borders** panel previews the actual market graph by year. In a resolved Domestic market, it lists only the accounts in that saved report. The number changes with the year and selected government. Nearby bilateral actors do not all belong to the domestic graph. A market point represents an exchange, not sovereignty over a drawn area. Census outlines are dated references rather than exact current-turn administrative boundaries. Public source builds continue to omit the historical outline layer while redistribution rights are unresolved.

Manipur and Tripura retain their existing accounts and works when they become states in 1972. Their balances are not reset. Each road clears at most one food unit in either direction in a year, with the existing food priority, reserve floor and buyer cash constraints. Households consume delivered relief; other deliveries enter stocks. Each delivered unit transfers money between the two governments. All three administrations can develop through the existing rival investment rules. Added markets appear on the next resolved turn of an older save; archived reports remain unchanged.

Harvests, food units, road capacities, AI development envelopes and investment prices are gameplay values. These small market accounts are not representations of a state's complete historical budget. The separately sourced playable-state fiscal model continues to govern player allocations. Market allocations now use period fiscal references where recorded, with explicit modeled shares and labeled missing periods. See the [AI fiscal notes](market-fiscal-research.md). Historical calibration and national balance review remain open.

## Bay of Bengal shipping

Port Blair enters Standard in 1950 with its own food balance, treasury and works. Calcutta carries up to three units and Madras one. The modeled capacities reflect relative service frequency. The 1950–51 Home Affairs report records regular service to both ports, a second charter for timber in 1950, and Union food distribution to the islands. The game treats Port Blair as an exchange for the islands and uses 1950 as the first year of modeled service. One routine Union food unit contributes to its balance each year. Light keeps an island account in the two coastal campaigns and their charter decisions. It omits wider market trade. [Home Affairs annual report, chapter on Andaman and Nicobar](https://www.mha.gov.in/files/annual_reports_documents/REPORT_1950_51_12022021.pdf#page=41)

From 1950 through 1952, West Bengal and Madras may commit one berth to a three-year food or timber charter. Both require market access. Food requires connected stores and departs only after home needs, ordinary trade and a four-month reserve minimum. It fills present gaps and up to two units of island reserves. The Union pays for each delivery. Timber requires an operating connected workshop and yields more receipts per load. The two paths share the berth and the contract opens no further award. Signing charges the displayed fee once. Following Andhra in 1953 removes access to the Madras charter. The standard sea route continues through the simulated Madras exchange. The brief shows what departed, what the state earned, and island food need after trade. The national chart marks the route and the ending records delivered cargo. Rates, floor, duration and shipment units are game assumptions. In 1952, Calcutta quotes ₹12 lakh for food and ₹18 lakh for timber. Madras quotes ₹8 lakh and ₹12 lakh. Food pays ₹3 lakh per unit and timber ₹7 lakh per load at those prices.

The island account uses reported revenue of ₹85.69 lakh, ₹79.292 lakh and ₹107.97 lakh in FY 1949–50 through FY 1951–52. The first two observations precede or coincide with entry. Later references use the FY 1972–73, 1978–79 and 1985–86 annual Plan outlays, kept separate from revenue receipts in the ledger. These anchors fund the existing modeled share. [Parliament, 16 December 1952](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2638332/1/01_II_16-12-1952_p21_p22_u699.pdf) · [Home Affairs 1972–73](https://www.mha.gov.in/sites/default/files/REPORT_1972_73_12022021.pdf) · [Home Affairs 1978–79](https://mha.gov.in/files/annual_reports_documents/REPORT_1978_79_12022021.pdf) · [Home Affairs 1986–87](https://mha.gov.in/files/annual_reports_documents/REPORT_1986_87_12022021.pdf)

Installed review path. Start West Bengal on the market programme in 1946, approve Bardhaman Rice and Reserves in 1949 and sign for 1952–54 from the Food & Water or Trade & Industry cabinet. Compare the same start with each charter. The food run protected home stores in 1952, then sent three units in each of the next two years. Port Blair's combined shortfall fell from ten to four while Bengal earned ₹18 lakh gross. The timber run shipped nine loads, earned ₹63 lakh gross and kept its early cash advantage after the charter fee. In a third run, Madras shipped one load and lost the berth after choosing Andhra. All three installed runs resumed a signed charter, reviewed the route and completed the 1985 ending. The desktop and narrow layouts were checked.

The original roughly one-to-four-hour human playtime goal cannot be established by automated turn advancement. Browser runs and financial checks provide evidence for stability, rather than evidence that the full game is fun or release-ready.

## Western coast shipping

**Goa, Daman and Diu** enter the domestic model in 1962, the first full year after the statutory appointed day of 20 December 1961. Mormugao already exported ore and had mechanical loading under Portuguese administration. Port Trust governance begins in 1964. The route models three rail slots shared between ore and domestic food. Ore occupies one until the 1979 terminal opens and two thereafter. The model reserves the remaining slots for food, including any interstate relief. A one-slot coastal link between Bombay and Panjim enters national clearing in 1963 based on the documented steamer service. It remains separate from the ore railway. [Administration Act §2](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1369/2/A1962-01.pdf) · [Port Authority history](https://mptgoa.gov.in/about-us/history) · [Lok Sabha, August 1963, Q937](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/2856/1/lsd_03_05_27-08-1963.pdf)

**Maharashtra's domestic freight** sees Mormugao exporters in the spot market from 1973. They compete for the same orders as Bombay and established inland carriers. The committee record dates the real diversion of sugar from southern Maharashtra and northern Mysore to November 1973 amid congestion at Bombay. The game assigns the exporting firms a modest bid and records their won loads separately from the Union territory's public treasury. Connected workshops and terminals already raise the player's competing capacity. This uses the existing freight rules and keeps the five-year Union contract separate. [Public Accounts Committee §§4.21–4.23](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/4105/1/pac_5_230_1977.pdf)

**Calicut and Kavaratti** connect from 1958 following the first documented experimental charter. The islands have a separate account from 1956 and use the Lakshadweep name from 1973. A second charter raises the modeled capacity from one to two in 1963. Heavy monsoon conditions remove one slot until the first all-weather ship arrives in 1970. Capacity rises to three that year and to four after the additional ship in 1982. The islands receive one modeled unit of rice from existing administration provisioning. Additional administration rice is prioritized aboard the Calicut vessel, with up to one planned load from 1958, two from 1963, and three from 1982. It comes from separately funded supplies. Food trade and interstate relief can use any remaining space. This enables the vessel to relieve food gaps when Kerala itself has little to sell. The independent account represents the archipelago and preserves its stocks through the renaming. The rice provisioning draws on the documented administration exchange for coir, with food as the modeled cargo. [Home Affairs 1957–58, p. 71](https://www.mha.gov.in/sites/default/files/REPORT_1957_58_12022021.pdf) · [Home Affairs 1966–67, §86](https://mha.gov.in/files/annual_reports_documents/REPORT_1966_67_12022021.pdf) · [Lok Sabha, July 1967](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/850938/1/04_II_05-07-1967_p107_p157_PII.pdf) · [Lakshadweep District Census Handbook, transport](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/670/download/2328/DH_2011_3101_PART_A_DCHB_LAKSHADWEEP.pdf)

**The fiscal ledger** quotes ₹501 lakh estimated Goa territorial revenue in 1962–63. Later Goa annual Plan references are ₹835 lakh in 1971–72, ₹980 lakh in 1972–73, ₹2,750 lakh in 1978–79 and ₹6,400 lakh in 1985–86. The 1972–73 report confirms ₹980 lakh in §4.88. For the islands, the corresponding Plan observations begin in 1972–73 at ₹45 lakh, then ₹244 lakh in 1978–79 and ₹765 lakh in 1985–86. The actual market allowances use the existing fraction for each measure. Route capacity, stocks, harvests, private bid sizes and allowances are game values. Standard politics runs the new accounts. Reports saved before the change keep their original data. [1962–63 budget speech, p. 404](https://eparlib.sansad.in/bitstream/123456789/239/1/Budget_Speech_Interim_1962-63.pdf) · [Home Affairs 1972–73, §§4.18 and 4.88](https://www.mha.gov.in/sites/default/files/REPORT_1972_73_12022021.pdf) · [Home Affairs 1978–79, Appendix I](https://mha.gov.in/files/annual_reports_documents/REPORT_1978_79_12022021.pdf#page=108) · [Home Affairs 1985–86, Annexure 2](https://mha.gov.in/files/annual_reports_documents/REPORT_1985_86_12022021.pdf)

In the installed Standard Bombay-to-Maharashtra run, rough weather cost the early Calicut service one slot in both 1963 and 1964. The 1963 brief recorded two units of unmet needs after deliveries and stocks. Mormugao won 13 spot loads across the last 13 turns. The 1979 map recorded the tighter food rail limit and the 1982 report showed three units shipped from Calicut. The campaign resumed after 1979 and reached 1985. The route cards and map were reviewed at desktop and narrow widths. These results show the routes working. A release balance pass will compare their influence on different player strategies.

## Pondicherry and the Villupuram railway

India took over the administration of Pondicherry, Karaikal, Mahé and Yanam on 1 November 1954. The separate annual market opens in the first full year, 1955. The 16 August 1962 cession changes the account's status to Union territory and preserves the reserves, treasury and works. In Standard, Pondicherry trades with Madras along the existing Villupuram railway. A player who follows Andhra sees the transfer and market from outside. A player who retains Madras can use the rail agreement in either preset. Mahé and Yanam remain distinct places in the transfer record. The trade route serves the Coromandel exchange and cannot act as a shortcut between the geographically separated enclaves. [1954 transfer agreement](https://legal.un.org/legislativeseries/pdfs/chapters/book17/book17_part1_I.pdf) · [1962 Administration Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/11234/1/the_pondicherry_%28administration%29_act%2C_1962.pdf) · [Railway and trade, 1981 Census Handbook, pp. 24–25](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/29898/download/33079/26920_1981_DCH.pdf)

The 1955 Madras decision draws on the loss of older textile export markets, the two mill closures and the contemporary proposal for two years of transitional concessions. A cabinet can **share food reserves** or **restart textile freight** for 1955 and 1956. On the first path, both sides can send supplies above a six-month home reserve floor to cover an actual shortfall. Emergency shipments and paid food trade share three slots. The Villupuram line carries the aid in either direction. On the second path, Madras pays a modeled ₹10 lakh once to reserve two slots for cloth. It earns ₹4 lakh per load, and a severe flood can leave one booked slot unused. One slot remains available for food. The choice is a negotiated scenario. Its load sizes and financial terms are gameplay values. Once the agreement expires, the regular three-slot food route resumes. [Nehru's August 1955 note](https://nehruarchive.in/documents/economic-and-cultural-issues-in-pondicherry-26-august-1955-25x8g) · [Historical Karaikal paddy and Cauvery dependence](https://cwc.gov.in/sites/default/files/report-cauvery-water-disputes-tribunal-volume-2.pdf)

The market ledger uses the recorded Annual Plan outlays of ₹73.96 lakh in FY 1955–56, ₹218 lakh in 1968–69, ₹264 lakh in 1970–71, ₹348.48 lakh in 1972–73, ₹1,090 lakh in 1978–79 and ₹3,300 lakh in 1985–86. It credits the existing small market share and shows the cited fiscal year. Textile freight payments belong to the player contract, recorded separately from Pondicherry's Plan allocation. The replacement pier opened in 1963, after the rail agreement's window. A distinct seaport connection remains outside this rail chapter. [Puducherry Planning Department](https://pandr.py.gov.in/sites/default/files/Sectoral%20Document%202019-2020.pdf) · [1968 Plan](https://www.publicationsdivision.nic.in/journals/Journalarchives/Yojana/Yojana-English/1968/August/Yojana_1968_August_pdf.pdf) · [1970 Plan](https://www.publicationsdivision.nic.in/journals/Journalarchives/Yojana/Yojana-English/1970/September/Yojana_1970_September_pdf.pdf) · [1972 Home Affairs](https://www.mha.gov.in/sites/default/files/REPORT_1972_73_12022021.pdf) · [1978 Home Affairs](https://mha.gov.in/files/annual_reports_documents/REPORT_1978_79_12022021.pdf#page=108) · [1985 Home Affairs](https://mha.gov.in/files/annual_reports_documents/REPORT_1985_86_12022021.pdf)

Two installed playthroughs used the default start with market links and early delta food investment. Both resumed the signed decision and reached the 1985 ending. The textile path dispatched one load after the 1955 flood and two the following year, earning ₹12 lakh before the ₹10 lakh fee. The reserve path kept its full capacity and delivered no emergency supplies in those two years. An independent sweep tested both options across 48 starting seeds, three investment paths and three food policies. In one matched 1955 case, the pact covered a one-unit Madras shortfall. In a second, Madras sent enough to cover Pondicherry's one-unit shortfall. The decision and route map were reviewed at desktop and narrow widths.

## Review path

1. Open the Campaign atlas, choose Standard and expand **Beyond your borders**. Change Market preview from 1946 to 1972. Manipur, Tripura and Arunachal appear in the later market list without changing the campaign date or save. Switch to Light to see the narrower scope.
2. Begin Assam, Standard, through 1985. Fund Food in 1946, Trade in 1948, Rail Link Feeders in 1950, Technical Schools in 1953, the Union-backed Brahmaputra agreement in 1956 and Industry in 1959. Resolve the historical chapters normally.
3. Check Neighbors after 1950, 1955 and 1972. Imphal, Agartala and Bomdila become direct partners at the recorded dates. Each road shows its one-unit annual food limit. Agreements and depots preserve that limit. Reload after the 1972 reorganization and continue through the ending.
4. In a Standard central campaign, resolve through 1972 and open Domestic market. The expanded northeastern schematic separates the seven market labels. Active and idle road routes identify their type and one-unit capacity. Saved-report coverage lists only that year's accounts. Review at desktop and narrow widths.
