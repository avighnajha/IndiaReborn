# The 1965–66 food crisis

The cabinet gets two decisions in successive dry years. It can draw down its own stores or pay for a limited Union delivery to meet needs and keep more food in reserve. Market links and working freight terminals increase the delivery limit. Under Standard politics the order uses capacity on the commercial spot freight market. Renewal takes a fresh decision the following year. A short campaign ending in 1965 sees the first year and the long campaign sees both.

The pilot covers Bihar, Northern Bihar after the split, and Orissa including merger with Mayurbhanj. Other administrations keep their ordinary weather. The in-game Turn 1965 covers the failed 1965 monsoon and the 1965–66 harvest, and Turn 1966 covers the second failed monsoon. The Union response is an alternate-history choice. Its terms and units are game values. The game applies rainfed harvest losses of 37% and 48% in Bihar and 31% and 37% in Orissa, with half the local rate on irrigated works. These are tuned simulation coefficients. Extension, water networks and funded food priorities can recover losses using the existing rules. Embankments protect against flood damage. The map highlights direct crop losses at irrigated sites before regional recovery.

An order authorizes delivery against the shortfall between harvest and local demand, up to the lesser of eight units and linked-town and freight-terminal capacity. Existing funds after funded services limit the order. The price uses the period index and a modeled ₹3 lakh opening-price basis per unit. The state pays once for food received, before ordinary market purchases. Every two units or part thereof use one commercial freight slot under Standard politics. Standing freight contracts are accounted for separately. Unmet need can still draw on the existing state reserve, shared reserves and emergency market purchases. The annual brief records actual quantities, stocks and costs. Archived reports keep their original outcomes.

Bihar's supplier branch served one commercial freight load per year with orders, down from two. Orissa's first order left its actual freight loads at two. Its second reduced loads from two to one. In Orissa the stock from the 1966 irrigated seed trials and the storage ceiling gave the local and Union branches the same closing stock, so preserving inventory was less valuable in that route. Under additional construction and seed 1947, food partnership portfolios still avoided reserve draws in both regions under both politics presets. The 1965 Northern Bihar supplier order under Light delivered three units and used no competitive freight. These results preserve the option to weather the drought with several portfolios.

The reserve route solves more of local need from its own farms and preserves reserves for later. A region with a strong balance and unused stores still has limited reason to invest deeper in food. The national market audit found both buyer funding constraints and physical bottlenecks. A later interstate contribution should use finite stocks and route headroom, fund deliveries to cash-constrained recipients explicitly, and report actual unmet need covered.

The installed Standard Bihar reserve campaign and Orissa supplier campaign reached 1985. The Orissa run reopened the 1965 order from its save, received the forecast quantities and made a fresh decision in 1966.
