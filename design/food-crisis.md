# The 1965–66 food crisis

In successive dry years the cabinet can draw down its own stores or pay for a limited Union delivery to meet needs and keep more food in reserve. Market links and working freight terminals increase the delivery limit. Under Standard politics the order uses capacity on the commercial spot freight market. Renewal takes a fresh decision the following year. A short campaign ending in 1965 sees the first year and the long campaign sees both.

The pilot covers Bihar, Northern Bihar after the split, and Orissa including merger with Mayurbhanj. Other administrations keep their ordinary weather. The in-game Turn 1965 covers the failed 1965 monsoon and the 1965–66 harvest, and Turn 1966 covers the second failed monsoon. The Union response is an alternate-history choice. Its terms and units are game values. An order authorizes delivery against the shortfall between harvest and local demand, up to the lesser of eight units and linked-town and freight-terminal capacity. Existing funds after funded services limit the order. The price uses the period index and a modeled ₹3 lakh opening-price basis per unit. The state pays once for food received, before ordinary market purchases. Every two units or part thereof use one commercial freight slot under Standard politics. Standing freight contracts are accounted for separately. Unmet need can still draw on the existing state reserve, shared reserves and emergency market purchases. The annual brief records actual quantities, stocks and costs. Archived reports keep their original outcomes.

## Interstate relief

The same drought plan lets any founding administration with connected stores offer food in 1965 or 1966. The Union can assign the offer to places with unmet needs after ordinary trade. The cabinet keeps six months of local demand in reserve where stocks allow, and can withdraw the offer from the map before advancing. It chooses whether to order food for home or make an outgoing offer. [Andhra Pradesh’s 1967 budget speech](https://apfinance.gov.in/pbs-1967-middle.html) records its historical central-pool contribution and shipments to Bihar even while the state was managing its own shortages. The recipients and terms below come from the alternate campaign.

Connected storage and towns limit what the cabinet can offer. State stores already drawn to meet local demand come out of eligible opening stocks. The model satisfies home needs and ordinary trade before dispatch, applies the offer to current shortages, then caps what the harvest can replace at home. No payment changes hands between the donor and recipient. The Union coordinates the relief. Under Standard politics, each route leg has to have capacity left and every two units or part thereof take one player spot freight slot. Long-running freight contracts retain their terms. Light politics uses the nearby markets and their route limits. Existing AI crop protection applies against regional drought losses in Bihar, Orissa and the eastern share of Uttar Pradesh. The market loss factors and all food units are simulation values.

The brief names the recipients, delivered amounts, remaining gaps and home stocks. The domestic market view shows relief legs with distinct dashed arrows and retains commercial shipment totals. The end-of-campaign Food & Water card counts supplies that reached a shortage. Existing reports keep their recorded balances.
