# Bombay, Maharashtra and Gujarat campaign sources

## Territorial changes and obligations

- The 1946 start uses the directly administered Bombay districts in the 1941 census layer. The 1949 chapter combines modeled Baroda and Deccan princely sites. The first full outline after that chapter uses the 1951 census. [Government of India White Paper on Indian States](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/White_Paper_on_Indian_States_(1950))
- The 1956 act brought Saurashtra, Kutch, and parts of the former Hyderabad and Madhya Pradesh states into Bombay. It also transferred southern districts to the new Mysore state. The game groups these changes in one planning turn and uses combined 1961 Maharashtra and Gujarat outlines for enlarged Bombay. The transferred hexes and the shaded historical outline represent different levels of map detail. [States Reorganisation Act, 1956, sections 7–8](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1680/1/a1956-37.pdf)
- The 1960 act formed Gujarat and renamed the remainder Maharashtra on 1 May. It allocated land and buildings by location and divided the cash balance by a 66.31 to 33.69 Maharashtra–Gujarat population ratio. The game applies that ratio to the treasury and uses it to estimate the split of reserves and modeled population. Projects stay at their original sites through transfer. Bombay and Ahmedabad are the respective map entry points after succession. [Bombay Reorganisation Act, 1960, sections 2–3 and 47–51](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/indiacode/handle/123456789/1535?view_type=browse) · [Ahmedabad district history](https://ahmedabad.gujarat.gov.in/assets/downloads/District_Disaster_Management_Plan.pdf)

## Regional economy

Programme prices, schedules and outcomes are designed for the campaign. Port projects draw on Kandla's development and Gujarat's textile markets. The Nagpur projects represent Vidarbha's inland cotton and engineering economy. Capital works are prepaid. On succession each government takes over maintenance for its inherited works. The other state uses inherited operating irrigation, storage, workshops and freight terminals in its food, cash and trade decisions.
