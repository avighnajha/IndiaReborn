The 1949 Government of India development review lists total West Bengal development expenditure of ₹165 lakh in FY 1947–48 from August 1947 onward, ₹722 lakh in the FY 1948–49 revised estimate, and ₹1,462 lakh in the FY 1949–50 original budget estimate. Of these totals, ₹13 lakh, ₹390 lakh, and ₹982 lakh respectively were classified as capital expenditure. The review warns that reductions in planned central loans would cut the 1949–50 programme. The three totals use different reporting bases. [Provincial Development Programme, Chapter XI, Tables I and II and footnotes](https://dspace.gipe.ac.in/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10973/39679/GIPE-022067.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=3)

The game uses the later West Bengal fiscal profile as a scale proxy for its 1946 start in the western part of undivided Bengal. The ₹44 lakh opening treasury comes from the modeled 1.3% opening allowance on the FY 1949–50 revenue reference. The initial ₹17 lakh annual works allowance follows the 0.5% envelope rule. Programme prices, operating receipts, and negotiated grants are game amounts. Extended play carries forward the latest available fiscal reference.

## Territory and chronology

- **1947 partition.** Section 3 of the Indian Independence Act establishes East Bengal and West Bengal from 15 August 1947. The Bengal Boundary Commission report published on 17 August supplies the demarcation. The campaign starts with the player managing the western area within undivided Bengal, then follows the creation of West Bengal. A player relief response is a modeled provincial decision. [Indian Independence Act, section 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Geo6/10-11/30/section/3/enacted) · [Bengal Boundary Commission report, Gazette of India](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Index%3AReport_of_the_Bengal_Boundary_Commission_%28Radcliffe_Award%29.pdf)
- **Cooch Behar.** The merger agreement is dated 28 August 1949 and transferred administration to the Dominion on 12 September. The States’ Merger (West Bengal) Order took effect on 1 January 1950 and placed it under West Bengal administration. The Cooch Behar district history records 19 January for the district’s emergence. Use the legal order date for the state-level annual transition. [Census of India 1951, Cooch Behar Merger Agreement](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/28172/download/31354/44330_1951_COO.pdf) · [States’ Merger (West Bengal) Order, 1949, Appendix XLVI](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/White_Paper_on_Indian_States_(1950)/Part_8/Orders_under_Section_290A_of_the_Government_of_India_Act#APPENDIX_XLVI) · [Supreme Court’s Berubari advisory opinion, p. 270](https://www.calcuttahighcourt.gov.in/Show-Judgment-File/1960~scr_1960_3_250_296_e.pdf) · [District history](https://coochbehar.gov.in/brief-history/)
- **Chandernagore.** The former French territory joined West Bengal on 2 October 1954. Its enclave is small at campaign scale and may be absorbed into the regional outline approximation. [Chandernagore (Merger) Act, 1954, sections 1 and 3](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/repealedfileopen?rfilename=A1954-36.pdf)
- **Bihar transfer.** On 1 November 1956, West Bengal received portions of Kishanganj subdivision and Gopalpur thana of Purnea district. The surveyed line was defined relative to the Dalkola–Kishanganj–Chopra–Siliguri and Dalkola–Karandighi–Raiganj highways. Section 3 initially added this northern territory to Darjeeling district. Most of Manbhum’s Purulia subdivision became West Bengal’s Purulia district in Burdwan division. Bihar retained Chas thana, Chandil thana, and Patamda police station of Barabhum thana. The 1957 planning turn is the first full turn after the transfer. [Bihar and West Bengal (Transfer of Territories) Act, 1956, sections 2–3](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1636/1/195640.pdf)

The census-year outlines approximate period jurisdiction. A 1941 boundary layer predates Partition and a 1951 layer reflects the Cooch Behar merger. The 1961 outline reflects the Bihar transfer. Map geometry, site ownership, and fiscal figures are tracked separately. See [map production](map-production.md) for digitization, attribution, and commercial redistribution status.

India recognized Bangladesh in December 1971. The game uses the Bangladesh name from the following planning year and preserves the eastern trade route's identity in saves. [Ministry of External Affairs, India–Pakistan relations documents](https://www.mea.gov.in/Images/pdf/India-Pakistan-std.pdf)

## Development context

- **Hooghly port and jute.** The Government of India’s 1949 development review identifies disruption to both raw jute and rice from the east after Partition. The 8 August 1949 Constituent Assembly debate describes the exposure of the West Bengal jute mills to supply disruption. The Calcutta port authority’s history documents the river port and its eastern Indian hinterland. Local raw-jute sourcing, mill adaptation, freight, and reserve investments draw on these conditions. The scale and delivery terms in the game are modeled. [Provincial Development Programme, Chapter XI](https://dspace.gipe.ac.in/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10973/39679/GIPE-022067.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=3) · [Constituent Assembly Debates, p. 248](https://bombayhighcourt.gov.in/bhc/libweb/misc/coi/constitution/assembleyDebate/pages/Page_1688469069_08-08-1949%20Page%20No%20242-261.pdf) · [Port authority history](https://smportkolkata.shipping.gov.in/smpk/en/brief-history-of-smpk/)
- **Damodar Valley.** The Damodar Valley Corporation Act of 1948 established the irrigation, power, and flood-control mandate. Sections 33–36 separate capital costs by purpose. The Centre, Bihar and West Bengal shared power capital equally. Each state paid for works used exclusively for its irrigation and shared common irrigation capital in proportion to guaranteed water off-takes. The Centre and West Bengal shared the first ₹14 crore of flood-control capital, with West Bengal liable for the excess. These distinctions provide historical context for alternative funding and operating choices. [Damodar Valley Corporation Act, 1948, sections 12 and 33–36](https://indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/1807/1/194814.pdf)
- **Technical education.** IIT Kharagpur dates its first operations to 1950, its move from temporary Calcutta premises to Hijli to September 1950, and its formal inauguration to 18 August 1951. The campaign’s own schools, colleges and research investment are modeled regional programmes. [IIT Kharagpur institutional history](https://www.iitkgp.ac.in/about-iitkgp-history)
