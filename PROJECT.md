# India Alternate Futures Project Record

Updated 24 November 2026. The original design and handoff in `design` describe the long-term scope. The [current design](design/current-design.md) reconciles them with later decisions. This file tracks feedback, priorities, and delivery.

## Product intent

**Start with the India that existed before independence. Build the India that could have existed instead.** The player chooses a historical region in 1946 and leads it through independence, changes of jurisdiction and decades of development. Terrain, water, transport and new works should show what the player made of that region. The [core thesis and sprint review criteria](design/current-design.md#core-thesis) guide assignments and reviews.

## Feedback and decisions

Roads now follow terrain, cross rivers on bridges, and use separate trade highlights. Higher terrain, settlement, animation and art fidelity remain release work. |

| Currency should reflect the real fiscal scale of the period. | Use nominal rupees in lakh and crore, with documented revenue anchors and a clearly labeled player-controlled development envelope. Keep source years, data status and boundaries with observations. |
| Let the player bargain for more central funding. | The Budget office offers matching funds. The Kosi Compact adds a choice between Bihar financing and earmarked Union co-funding with a longer delivery schedule. Grant and agreement terms are scenario mechanics with period precedent. |
| Placing individual roads and irrigation still feels granular and boring after play. | The cabinet now approves multi-year regional programmes. The works office chooses and stages the individual sites. Existing works remain visible and continue to affect the campaign. |
| Gameplay feels dry and confusing. Construction choices feel arbitrary. | The opening briefing compares three provincial priorities with different costs, delivery dates, and follow-on opportunities. The map previews the full footprint. Players decide whether to protect reserves or pay to accelerate a programme. |
| Bihar and Orissa need recognizable geography and state shapes. | Both maps now use public elevation data, census-era outlines, approximate town coordinates and sourced major waterways. The Bihar–West Bengal transfer and Orissa's early mergers are visible on the map. The sources and commercial release requirements are recorded in the [map notes](design/map-production.md). |
| State borders need to be more distinct, with greater weight on the governed state. | The governed outline is bold gold in every layer. Neighboring census-era borders use a lighter line. The Borders view increases contrast. |
| Prioritize visuals and avoid an excessive testing process. | Use production-build captures and focused player flows for acceptance. Keep automated checks around money, saves and other rules where a regression would be hard to spot. |
| The annual report is hard to read at a glance. | Annual brief leads with the outcome, investment effects and a relevant next decision. Treasury and programme milestones stay visible. Earned receipts and their year-to-year change distinguish operating activity from the public allocation. The ledger opens under **Why this happened**. |
| Grain feels too prominent, and rain seems disconnected from the outcome. | The dashboard and brief lead with food security. The report separates harvest potential, weather damage, protection, reserves and purchases. Check the presentation before adjusting policy or balance. |
| Investment outcomes are hard to trace, especially technical schools. | The annual brief shows school openings, skill gains, industry contributions and follow-on proposals. Industry measures workshop output. Regional GDP remains a separate design question. |
| Decisions are verbose, and returning from the annual report makes a pending decision hard to find. | The regional map names the pending decision and offers a Review button. Decision cards use a treasury cost and short bullets for effects. |
| Deep zoom softens text and built projects are hard to read. | Labels use a sharper, capped scale. Wheel zoom follows the cursor. The Works view and site inspector distinguish operating and unfinished projects. |
| It is unclear what neighbors simulate and how the economy feeds the treasury. | Neighbors model harvests, stock, trade and migration. The Economy breakdown and annual ledger separate workshop and market receipts from the public allocation. |
| Choosing investments often changes only their order. Each department needs several levels like a strategy game's technology tree. | Founding campaigns have four tiers each in Education, Food & Water, and Trade & Industry. Education has two charter-linked capstones. All three draw from the same treasury and works office. Maps and annual briefs show the works and their effects. |
| Development tree cards are too verbose. Give each type of work a recognizable icon across states. | Each card leads with a facility icon, a common label and a short effect. Review the card to see the local programme, footprint and prerequisites. |
| The link between Regional programmes and Development trees is unclear. The annual brief needs more visual variety. | The cabinet, trees, map previews and annual brief share department colours and labels. Programme details link to the relevant tree node. The brief leads with food coverage, the monsoon, the treasury and changes since last year. |
| Programme names and locations are specific to Bihar. | Bihar and Orissa share the programme rules and have distinct portfolios, geography and choices. Orissa's Hirakud agreement adds a power allocation decision that changes later production. |
| Keep development aligned with the game's alternate-history thesis. | Prioritize consequential regional choices. Assign and review work against the decision it improves and what changes on the map, in the brief or at the ending. |

## Version control

Commit each verified milestone to the playable project repository and push its source to IndiaReborn. Use [the source exporter](tools/export_public_source.py) on the playable checkout's committed HEAD until the exact historical boundaries are cleared or replaced. The export preserves the terrain and campaign logic and uses placeholders for the outline layer. Review the staged public diff, then build and run checks before pushing. The original Word design stays in the playable checkout. See the [map notes](design/map-production.md).

## Next milestones

### Next gameplay sprint

Make regional investment change the long campaign. A matched Standard Bihar comparison through 1985 found distinct early results and convergence by the ending. All three openings approved 14 programmes and met both original goals by following the same purchasing rule without optional priorities or mandates. [Comparison and review scope](design/late-campaign-balance.md#matched-opening-review)

The [Union partnership comparison](design/union-partnerships.md) delivers this choice in Bihar and Orissa. The reserve route protects food supplies in bad monsoons. The supplier route turns earlier education and market work into production and later freight wins. The [mid-1960s drought](design/food-crisis.md) now tests both investments. A cabinet can draw on stores, pay for limited deliveries where available, or commit surplus stocks to actual shortages along its trade network. An independent review compared held and offered reserves across 48 annual resolutions. It checked conservation, route limits and the range of freight trade-offs, including years where spare capacity and a good surplus harvest absorb the entire cost. The map, annual brief and ending show where the offer landed. Record human playtester feedback on whether these consequences are easy to anticipate and explain.

Bring forward food and freight pricing when it influences one of those choices. The broader fiscal and national coverage work continues under the release milestones. Research, UI and implementation assignments should each identify the decision they serve and the evidence needed for review.
